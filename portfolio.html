Spatial & Temporal Distribution of NO₂ Over Lahore

Final Year Thesis

Integrated Sentinel-5P/TROPOMI satellite data (via Google Earth Engine) with ground-based Punjab EPA monitoring across ten spatial slices of Lahore to map nitrogen dioxide pollution from 2021–2025 — identifying consistent hotspots, confirming a strong seasonal pattern, and testing how well freely-available satellite data agrees with surface-level ground measurements. Co-authored with Amna Amin and Awais Javaid, supervised by Dr. Irfan Jalees, Institute of Environmental Engineering & Research, UET Lahore.

Methodology

Lahore was divided into ten spatial slices for zone-level comparison. Satellite NO₂ column-density data was extracted via Google Earth Engine (COPERNICUS/S5P/OFFL/L3_NO2) for each slice, and merged with Punjab EPA ground-station readings. Spatial hotspot mapping and cartography were carried out in QGIS; statistical comparison (ANOVA, Pearson correlation, RMSE, MAE) was run in Excel and Minitab.

Key takeaway

NO₂ hotspots were consistently concentrated in Mughalpura, Samanabad, and Model Town, while Shahdara and Iqbal Town remained consistently cleaner across all five years — a stable spatial pattern useful for targeting interventions. Raiwind Road and Samanabad showed the strongest satellite–ground agreement (lowest RMSE), while Gulberg and Iqbal Town showed the highest error, likely reflecting complex urban emission sources that column-density satellite data can't fully resolve. Overall, satellite data reliably identifies where pollution is worse across a data-scarce city, even where ground-monitoring infrastructure is limited, but shows only moderate agreement with ground-level readings in absolute terms.
